# 2026-08-11.6 — tall comments scroll, they don't skip

Commit: (this one)

## What changed

The tall-block walk (2026-08-10.22) treats a block taller than the viewport as
one focus stop you scroll through `tallWalkStep` lines at a time, moving focus
on only at the block's far edge. It was built over `m.spans`, which carries
*entry*-level rows — so a *comment* never got the treatment: the dive into a
thread walks comments, and each comment's rows live in `m.subspans`, the
comment-level map the same render pass registers. A large comment got the plain
dive walk: one `j` jumped straight from its head to the next comment, and at a
thread's ends one `j`/`k` spat you out of the thread entirely — the "jarring"
exit the feedback names.

`moveFocus`'s dive branch now applies the same tall-walk to the focused
comment, reading `m.subspans` (F5's one-pass rule: the render already knows
where each comment landed, the walk just reads it):

- While focus sits on a comment whose subspan is taller than the viewport,
  `j`/`k` scroll the viewport `tallWalkStep` lines at a time (3, the same step
  J/K, the wheel and the block walk use) and never move focus.
- Only once the viewport reaches the comment's far edge — its last row at the
  viewport bottom, or its head at the top — does the next press move focus to
  the next comment.
- Landing on a tall comment opens at the edge the walk enters from: its head
  walking down, its bottom walking up, with `scrollAnchor` pinned so the next
  render's `clampScroll` keeps the walked offset instead of re-deriving it
  (which would drop into the middle or pin the tail). `dive()` itself does the
  same for a thread whose *first* comment is tall — it opens at the comment's
  head rather than the tail `clampScroll` would otherwise pin.

The dive's flow-out at the ends is unchanged — a thread still never traps — but
it now fires only after the last comment's tail (or the first comment's head)
has actually been read, so leaving a thread happens by finishing it, not by one
accidental press.

**What I chose and rejected:** reusing `m.subspans` over a new per-comment
layout pass (the render already registers each comment's span; F5's rule is to
derive everything from that one pass), matching the block walk's 3-line step and
entering-edge landing exactly (one treatment for every tall thing, rather than a
separate rule for comments), keeping the dive's flow-out-at-the-ends over
trapping inside a thread (trapping re-creates the "a `j` that goes nowhere"
complaint the flow-out was built to fix — the tall-walk is what removes the
*accidental* exit), and a `!m.visual` guard mirroring the block walk's shape
(visual mode already lifts comment focus in `selection.start`, so the guard is
unreachable in the dive branch — belt-and-braces only).

## Verification

`make check` green (build, test, vet); `make test-race` green too — the leg
mutates navigation state the render goroutine reads, so the race gate was run
even though nothing here touches the composer. New tests in
internal/review/tall_test.go (`TestMoveFocusTallComment*`,
`TestDiveIntoTallCommentLandsAtItsHead`): landing on a tall comment opens at its
head walking down and its bottom walking up; j/k scroll the viewport
`tallWalkStep` at a time inside it and never move focus; the walked offset
survives `clampScroll`; the walk moves on only at the tail; diving a thread
whose first comment is tall opens at its head; and a render-path test drives a
real thread whose middle comment is taller than the viewport end to end.

## For the maintainer to look at

Whether a comment taller than the viewport should read via the 3-line scroll as
built — versus, say, a page-size step for very long comments — whether landing
at the entering edge (head walking down, bottom walking up) is the right way to
open a huge comment from a dive, and whether "leave only at the far edge" is the
wanted reading of the jarring-exit complaint (the thread's ends still flow out,
just not accidentally).

## Demo

```bash
make build
printf '# Doc\n\nFirst paragraph.\n\nSecond paragraph.\n\nThird paragraph.\n' > /tmp/tallthread.md
./bin/margin /tmp/tallthread.md
```

1. **Post a long comment.** `j` onto "First paragraph.", then `c`. The composer
   opens in insert mode. Paste a long paragraph — a sentence repeated until it
   wraps well past a screenful (your terminal's paste: `ctrl+shift+v`). Submit
   with `ctrl+s`.
2. **Dive and feel the walk.** `l` dives into the thread — focus lands on your
   comment at its head. Press `j`. I chose the same 3-line scroll a tall block
   gets: the viewport moves down three rows and focus stays on the comment,
   instead of the old behaviour of jumping straight past it out of the thread.
   Keep pressing `j` — it scrolls through the comment, and only when the
   comment's last row reaches the bottom of the terminal does the next `j`
   move focus on. Is 3 lines the right reading speed for a long comment, or
   does a very long comment want a bigger step?
3. **Walk back up.** `k` scrolls up 3 lines at a time to the comment's head,
   then moves focus back to the thread row. Does tail-to-head re-entry feel
   right?
4. **The thread no longer spits you out.** Note that while you were inside the
   comment no `j`/`k` moved you out of the thread — you leave only after
   reading to the end. That is the change; is it the wanted reading of
   "accidentally navigating out is jarring"?

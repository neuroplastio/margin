## What changed

The feedback asked for incremental viewport scrolling using `J`/`K` (or similar) to allow scrolling the view without changing focus.

- `move.scrollDown` and `move.scrollUp` added to the command registry (`internal/review/command.go`).
- Bound to `J` and `K` in `keymap`.
- Added `scrollViewport(delta int)` to `model` (`internal/review/review.go`) which shifts `m.scroll` directly by 3 lines per press without touching focus `m.at`.
- Because `m.at` stays equal to `m.scrollAnchor`, `clampScroll` preserves the user-driven scroll offset, allowing the viewport to move independently of focus.

`make check` green (build, test, vet); `make test-race` green.

## Demo

Command:
```bash
./bin/margin vault/goals.md
```

Keys to press:
1. Press `J` several times to scroll down the document viewport. Note that focus remains on the initial block while the viewport moves down.
2. Press `K` to scroll back up.
3. Press `j` or `k` to move focus again, observing how focus movement re-engages scroll anchoring.

Questions to answer:
- Does 3 lines per `J`/`K` keypress feel like the right step size, or should it be faster/slower?
- Is scrolling the viewport without shifting focus clear and intuitive?

Chosen vs rejected:
- **Chosen:** 3 lines per press, matching the default mouse wheel step (`delta = 3`).
- **Rejected:** 1 line per press (too slow for page scanning) and 5+ lines (too jarring).

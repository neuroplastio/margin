# 2026-08-08.8 — RENDER-07: frontmatter's visual treatment

Kind: felt
Task: RENDER-07
Commit: (this one)

## What changed

No open questions blocked the board and `vault/feedback/` was empty, so this
is the next unblocked M1 backlog item — the last one in it, in fact; `Backlog
— M1` is now empty.

PARSE-03 gave frontmatter its own `blockFrontmatter` kind so goldmark could
not misparse it as a thematic break plus a setext heading, but left its
rendering undecided: `rebuild()` filtered it out of `m.entries` entirely, so a
document that opened with `---\n...\n---` showed nothing where that block was.
Invisible was a default landed to unblock the rest of parsing, not a
decision — the maintainer's feedback later offered three real candidates
(hidden-on-demand, a dimmed key/value block, a collapsed line that expands)
and none was picked. This leg picks one and builds it.

**What I chose: a dimmed key/value block, always visible, indented to match
the gutter.** `frontmatterFields` (`internal/review/parse.go`) strips the
leading and trailing `---` fence lines from the block's raw text and returns
the inner lines, trimmed of trailing whitespace but otherwise untouched —
nested YAML (a `tags:` list, say) keeps its own indentation rather than being
flattened. `renderFrontmatter` (`internal/review/review.go`) styles each field
`dimStyle` and prepends `gutterW` spaces so it lines up with the prose below
it, then a blank line. `render()` calls it directly against `m.doc[0]` — not
through `m.entries` — since `parseDoc` always puts a frontmatter block first
when one exists, and it stays out of the entry list entirely: no focus stop,
no `c`, no `space`. `rebuild()`'s existing filter (`b.kind ==
blockFrontmatter → continue`) is unchanged; the block just gets a renderer
called on it before the entry loop starts instead of nothing at all.

**What I rejected, and why.** Hidden-on-demand and a collapsed line that
expands both need a new key or a new focus/expansion state to become visible
at all — the same shape of decision CMD-03's palette or THREAD-02's
resolved-thread question are still waiting on, and neither is settled yet.
The dimmed block needs none of that: it costs a few rows at the top of every
document and nothing else. A reviewer scanning `status: draft` or `tags:`
before reading the prose is the common case a felt leg should optimise for,
not the rare one "hidden until asked" assumes. If the maintainer judges it too
loud, cutting it down to a collapsed line is a smaller change than building
the expand/collapse machinery from scratch would have been to hedge against
that in advance.

A field wider than the measure is truncated with an ellipsis rather than
wrapped: frontmatter reads as key: value pairs, not prose, so a wrapped
continuation line would look like a second field rather than a spillover of
the first.

## Verification

`make check` (build, `go test ./...`, `go vet ./...`) is green. `gofmt -l`
clean. Not a composer or emulator change, so `make test-race` was not run.

`TestParsedFrontmatterDoesNotRenderOrCrash` pinned the *old* behaviour —
frontmatter must never appear on screen — and is now
`TestParsedFrontmatterRendersDimmedAndDoesNotCrash`, asserting the opposite
(the fields do appear) while keeping its other half unchanged: a
`blockFrontmatter` block must never reach `m.entries`, and rendering it must
not hit `render()`'s default branch (built for a thread entry, and it would
misbehave given a bare block). New tests: `TestFrontmatterFieldsStripsFences`
(fences dropped, nested indentation kept), `TestFrontmatterFieldsTooShortIsNil`
(a body with no closing fence reachable does not panic),
`TestRenderFrontmatterTruncatesLongFields` (a field past the measure is cut to
exactly the measure width, ellipsis included, not wrapped or left
overflowing), `TestRenderFrontmatterEmptyIsNil` (an empty YAML body between the
fences renders nothing rather than a bare blank line).

I cannot see a screen, so I cannot confirm the dimmed block actually reads as
"this is metadata, skip it" rather than as the document's own first
paragraph, that the indentation lines it up convincingly with the heading and
prose below, or that dim-on-dim (frontmatter dimmed sitting right above a
document that has not been reviewed yet, so its prose is bright) reads as
intentional rather than confusing. I did print `render()`'s de-styled output
for `testdata/sample.md` to sanity-check the arithmetic — five fields, each
indented three spaces, one blank line, then the real heading — but that
confirms the layout, not how it looks in a real terminal. See the demo
recipe.

No question raised.

Answer:

- Does the dimmed block read as metadata to skip past, or does it look like
  the document's first paragraph — something you might try to focus, mark, or
  comment on?
- Is the indentation (three spaces, matching the gutter width) enough to line
  it up with the heading and prose below, or does it need its own visual
  separator — a rule, extra blank space — to read as a distinct section?
- Press `j` from the top of the document. Focus should skip straight to the
  heading, with no stop on the frontmatter block at all — is that the right
  call, or does "I can see it but can't do anything to it" feel like a bug
  rather than a deliberate non-interactive treatment?
- Resize the terminal narrow and re-open, or try a document with a long
  `description:` field. Does the ellipsis truncation read as "there's more
  here" or does it just look cut off?
- What I chose: a dimmed key/value block, always visible, no new key or
  interaction needed to see it. What I rejected: hidden-on-demand and a
  collapsed-line-that-expands, both of which need new interaction machinery
  neither CMD-03 nor THREAD-02 has settled the shape of yet — the always-on
  dim treatment gets the whole thing built and judged in one leg instead of
  two.

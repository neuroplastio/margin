# 2026-08-10.25 — link navigation between blocks, with a jumplist

Kind: felt
Task: M3 navigation item "Link navigation between blocks, with a jumplist
(`ctrl+o` / `ctrl+i`)" — follow an in-document `[text](#heading-slug)` link in
the focused block to the heading it names, and walk back and forward through
those jumps with vim's two jumplist keys.
Commit: (this one)

## What changed

A new `jump.go` (internal/review) holds the jumplist and the follow verb;
`command.go` registers the three commands; `parse.go`'s `inlineRun` now
carries a link's `href` (it previously threw the URL away after stripping the
markup); `rebuild()` builds the heading-slug index alongside the entry list.

- **`jump.follow` (`ctrl+]`)** follows the first in-document link in the
  focused block — one whose href is a `#fragment` — resolving it against the
  document's headings via a GitHub-style slug (`## Retry policy` →
  `#retry-policy`, duplicates get `-1`, `-2`, … like GitHub) and landing
  focus on that heading, centred in the viewport like a search jump.
  - A block with no link reports `no link here`; a block whose links all
    point outside the document (an external URL, a relative file) reports
    `links here point outside this document` — cross-document following is
    the tree-view milestone's job — and a fragment that names no heading is
    surfaced, not silently dropped (`no heading matches #x`).
  - Links are read out of the block's prose — paragraphs, headings, list
    items, quotes, table cells — through the same `parseInline` the renderer
    uses, so what is navigable is exactly what renders as an underlined link.
- **The jumplist** (`ctrl+o` older, `ctrl+i` newer) is a capped (100) list of
  entries with a current-position marker. Every *teleport* verb records where
  it landed: link-follow, search jumps (`gotoMatch`), source-line jumps
  (`jumpToLine`), and the `goto` section command. The walkers (`j`/`k`, page
  keys, `g`/`G`) deliberately do not — recording those would flood the list
  the way recording `gg` every time would flood vim's. Each recorded jump
  also keeps the position it left behind, so `ctrl+o` returns to where the
  jump started and `ctrl+i` returns to where it landed; jumping from the
  middle of the list truncates the forward branch (vim's rule); a jump that
  leaves and lands on the same entry does not stack. A walk onto a stale
  entry (the document changed) clamps to the entry list.

**What I chose and rejected:** `ctrl+]` for follow, over `gf`/`gx` (both
`g`-prefixed, and `g` is an eager prefix that already moves to the first
block, so a two-key combo would fire that side effect first) and over
`enter`/`l` (dive is settled); recording search/line/section jumps in the
jumplist as well as link-follows (a jumplist that only knew link jumps would
make ctrl+o useless for the search hops a reviewer makes constantly, and
vim's own jumplist records those) while leaving the walkers out (flooding);
the GitHub slug rule including its `q--a`-style double hyphens (matching what
an agent's links actually name beats prettier slugs); first-link-wins in a
block with several (the block is the unit of focus, and following the first
is the one rule that needs no explanation).

## Notes

- `ctrl+i` is delivered as Tab on terminals that do not report the modifier —
  the same F16 caveat as `ctrl+enter`. On Ghostty (kitty protocol) it is its
  own key; `ctrl+o` and `ctrl+]` are always distinct control chars.
- On-disk formats and anchors are untouched; this is pure navigation. Nothing
  here changes what a link renders like — the underlined `linkStyle` was
  already RENDER-06's — only that it is now followable.

## Verification

`make check` green (build, test, vet). New tests in internal/review/jump_test.go:
slug generation, heading-slug index building with duplicate disambiguation,
follow to a heading (with centring and scrollAnchor), the three no-op
follows (no link / external-only / no matching heading), jumplist truncation
of forward history, dedupe, cap, back/forward walking to the ends, follow
recording a returnable source, search and line jumps landing in the list, and
the three keys routing through the registry.

## For the maintainer to look at

Whether `ctrl+]` is the follow key you want (the alternatives — `gf`/`gx`,
or making `enter` on a linked block follow — are rejected above and cheap to
swap), whether `ctrl+o`/`ctrl+i` walking a list that also contains search and
line jumps feels right, and whether first-link-wins is the rule you want for
a block with several links.

## Demo

```bash
make build
cat > /tmp/links.md <<'EOF'
# Review links

Read [the policy](#retry-policy) and the [revision](#retry-policy-1) — or
the [external docs](https://example.com).

## Retry policy

Backoff is exponential.

## Retry policy

Jitter is full.
EOF
./bin/margin /tmp/links.md
```

1. **Follow a link:** with focus on the paragraph under "Review links",
   press `ctrl+]` — focus jumps to the first `## Retry policy` heading and the
   viewport centres it. I chose `ctrl+]` (vim's "jump to the reference under
   the cursor") over `gf`/`gx` (both `g`-prefixed, and `g` is already the
   eager first-block key) and over `enter`/`l` (dive). Does `ctrl+]` feel
   right for this? Is it discoverable?
2. **The jumplist:** `ctrl+o` returns to the paragraph you followed from;
   `ctrl+i` returns to the heading. Then `42gg` (or `/` search), and `ctrl+o`
   walks back through *that* jump too — I recorded search and line jumps as
   well as link-follows so ctrl+o is useful beyond pure link hops. Do you
   want `j`/`k` or `g`/`G` recorded too, or is teleport-only right?
3. **Duplicate headings:** the second `## Retry policy` is `#retry-policy-1`
   (GitHub's rule). `ctrl+]` from the paragraph follows the first; move focus
   to the second heading and edit the paragraph link to `#retry-policy-1` to
   reach it. Is the `-1` disambiguation what you expected?
4. **Dead ends:** on a block with no link, `ctrl+]` reports `no link here`; on
   the external-docs link it reports `links here point outside this
   document`. Are those statuses the right signals, or should external links
   be followed too (that is the tree-view milestone, not this one)?

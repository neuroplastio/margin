## What changed

`V` now starts a **blockwise visual selection** — the maintainer's `Shift+V`
ask; a terminal delivers that as `V`. Movement of any kind extends it, because
the range is never stored, only derived: anchored at the entry where `V` was
pressed, running to wherever focus is now (`selectionRange`, `review.go`).
`esc` or a second `V` cancels, and any command that is not movement or
selection itself ends it — vim's rule, so no bookkeeping about which verbs
keep a selection alive. `y` copies the selection's **markdown source** to the
clipboard and cancels; with no selection active it yanks the focused block
alone, the degenerate range of one, so there is one code path.

Decisions made, and what they replaced:

- **Blockwise, not linewise.** j/k in visual mode walks entries only and never
  dives into a thread's comments; entering visual lifts a comment-level focus
  onto its block. The feedback says "threads remain line-based" — the
  selection unit is the block, and thread entries inside a range are the
  conversation, not the document: no highlight, not counted, not yanked.
- **`V` was taken.** `thread.showDeleted` held it since 2026-08-09.9 — an
  agent-chosen, still-unjudged binding, and that leg's own demo asked "is `V`
  a reasonable key?" The maintainer's newer feedback answers by reclaiming it.
  The reveal toggle moved to **`T`** (tombstones) and keeps its palette entry;
  the "comments deleted — T to reveal" hint moved with it.
- **The highlight is a painted background, and it had to be hand-rolled.** A
  dark-slate background (`236`) on every text line of a selected block, a
  blue (`111`) `▌` in the gutter of selected-but-unfocused blocks, the focus
  pink kept on the moving endpoint, and a `-- VISUAL -- n block(s)` mode line
  in the footer. Rejected: a gutter bar alone (the hover feedback already
  judged a lone dim `▌` "not visible enough"), and wrapping lines in a
  lipgloss background style — lipgloss does not reassert an outer style after
  an inner one resets, so chroma code and inline markup would lose the
  background mid-line. `selLine` instead reasserts the background after every
  inner reset (`\x1b[m` / `\x1b[0m`), one uniform treatment over plain text,
  inline markup and chroma alike; the test pins it against real chroma output.
- **The clipboard gets source, not the rendered view.** `blockSource`
  (`export.go`) is quoteBlock's dual — same per-kind reconstruction, no `> `
  prefix, no truncation: headings get their `#`s back, quotes their `>`
  markers, code its fence and language, tables and list items their verbatim
  source lines. A yank is a paste into something that reads markdown; gutter
  rules and highlights would be garbage there. Delivery is export's dual path
  (helper if installed, OSC 52 always).

## Verification

`make check` green (build, test, vet). No composer or emulator change —
selection state lives in the host's Update/render paths, not the pty-writer /
render-reader pair — so `make test-race` is not required by the gate.

New tests (`visual_test.go`): V anchors and j extends the range, esc and a
second V cancel, j/k never dives into comments mid-selection (and entering
visual lifts comment focus to its block), a non-movement command (`r`) ends
the selection, the render carries the background on selected blocks only
(threads inside the range stay unpainted, the focused endpoint keeps the pink
bar), `selLine` reasserts the background after every inner reset of real
chroma output, `blockSource` per kind against `testdata`-style source, `y`
yanks the focused block with no selection up, yank ends visual, and the
V/T/y keymap pinning. The one existing test that named the old binding
("V to reveal") moved to T with the string.

What I cannot verify: whether the `236` background plus blue gutter bar reads
as *selected* at a glance or fights the `212` focus pink, whether the mode
line is noticed, and whether yank's what-you-get matches what you thought you
chose — that is the demo below.

## Demo

```
make build && ./bin/margin testdata/sample.md
```

1. `j` a few times, then `V` — the focused block should pick up the dark
   background and the footer should read `-- VISUAL -- 1 block(s)`. `j`/`k` —
   the highlight grows and shrinks block by block, the count follows, and the
   endpoint you are standing on keeps the pink bar.
2. Select across a thread (somewhere with a collapsed `│` line between
   paragraphs) — the thread line stays unhighlighted while the blocks around
   it paint. Right or wrong?
3. `y`, then paste somewhere (`ctrl+shift+v` in a terminal, or `p` in nvim) —
   you should get the blocks' markdown source: `#`s on headings, `>` on
   quotes, fenced code with its language. Is source the right thing to paste,
   or did you want the rendered text?
4. No selection: `y` on a lone paragraph yanks just it. `V` then `esc`, or
   `V` then `V`, or `V` then `r` — all three cancel.
5. `T` still toggles deleted-comment visibility (was `V`).

Questions: does the dark-slate background + blue bar read as "selected"
against the pink focus bar, or do the two colours fight? Is blockwise (never
stopping on comments, threads invisible to the selection) the right
granularity for what you select in practice? Chose painted background +
gutter bar + footer mode line, over gutter-bar-only (hover was already too
quiet) and over reverse video (kills chroma and inline colours mid-line).
